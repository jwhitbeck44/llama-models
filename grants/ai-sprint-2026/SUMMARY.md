## Decisions taken and why

- Keep the build-and-test project. No found course combines AI-built test versions, required people outside the class, and measured results in an introductory undergraduate course. The closest precedent (Blank's Stanford Lean LaunchPad) produced a warning about AI-built products, and the Founder Task rubric is the course's answer to it.
- AI builds the artifact only. The student writes the claim, who the people are, and what counts as failure before anything is built, recruits the people, and reads the result. Reason: learning fell in the causal studies when AI replaced the student's reasoning (Bastani et al.; Lehmann et al.), and AI advice hurt weaker entrepreneurs in the only field trial (Otis et al.).
- The AI critique moved from an individual step to the Session 13 Machine Shop (one objection prompt on one student's page, on the projector). Reason: it stays in the existing format, adds no individual AI assignment, and is not a measured component.
- Module 13 changes from a task done in the room to a field task like Modules 2, 3, 4 and 6, scored on the Founder Task rubric extended to it. The rubric and syllabus currently exclude Phase 3 tasks from that rubric; the proposal says the extension plainly.
- Sign-up forms run in Microsoft Forms under the UCCS Microsoft 365 license, collecting a name and email only, deleted at term end. Reason: a public vulnerability record for AI-built sites, and the need to hold real people's data in a university system. Marked [CONFIRM with OIT].
- Measures: count of people outside the class reached (log checked against records); share of Final Pitches citing evidence from people outside the class, with what counts defined (Board members and AI output do not); blind rescoring of both cohorts' pitches in one window by three raters from neither cohort, with an ICC and a cohort-guess check; the unaided Final Exam Framework Application section; the AI Check-In survey in weeks 1 and 15 by major and first-generation status. Founder Task scores are not an outcome, because the rubric rewards an honestly described failure.
- Blind rescoring moved to one window in December 2027. The old plan scored Fall 2026 in spring and Fall 2027 in December, so raters would have known which batch was which.
- No within-semester randomized arm. With 21 per arm it detects only 0.89 SD and it splits the Boards. The proposal names the cohort comparison's limits and frames the project as a pilot reporting effect sizes with confidence intervals.
- Novelty narrowed to what the report supports: new in setting (introductory, many non-business majors, solo ventures) and measurement; not new in the build step, building with AI, or requiring real people. Inclusion is a hypothesis, reported by major and background.
- EPIIC appears once, as The Garage at UCCS, the place students whose test finds demand are referred. Its site lists mentoring and lab access and no test-user pool or showcase. The Venture Attractor has nothing for students and is not mentioned.
- References shortened to fit three pages; Koo and Li, Blank 2011, Fisher et al., Yu et al. and the 2024 Otis working paper were cut for space.
